HAND CONTROL

Mac: open Hand Control.app. Click Start Camera and allow Camera access.
Click Allow Accessibility, then enable Hand Control in System Settings >
Privacy & Security > Accessibility. If it is not listed, add the app with +.
If macOS asks you to reopen it, quit and reopen the same copy. You can
preview and calibrate without Accessibility, but the pointer will not move.
If you intend to move the app into Applications, do that before granting access.
If the switch is on but the app says "Preview only", first quit and reopen
the same copy, even if macOS did not ask. Look for "Desktop control enabled".
If it stays blocked after a rebuild, click Show This App. In Accessibility
remove just the old Hand Control entry, click +, and select that exact app
from Finder. Enable it and reopen Hand Control.
If the signing entry is still stale, quit the app and, only if you want to
clear this app's Accessibility approval, run:
  tccutil reset Accessibility local.handcontrol.desktop
Open the same app, click Allow Accessibility, approve its fresh request,
then quit and reopen it. This reset does not grant access. No Input
Monitoring or Full Disk Access is needed.

Windows 10/11 x64: extract the entire Windows ZIP, then double-click
Hand Control.exe inside the extracted folder. Keep the .dll and .task
files beside the EXE. No Python, download, account, or administrator access
is needed to run it. Click Start Camera. If access is blocked, click Camera
Settings and allow camera access for desktop apps. The Windows build is not
publisher-signed; check your device's software policies before running it.
Windows N editions need Microsoft's Media Feature Pack for webcam capture.

YOUR FIRST MINUTE
1. Choose your camera from the menu (built-in, external 720p/60fps webcam, or
   iPhone Continuity Camera on Mac) and face it in good light. The camera image
   is mirrored like a mirror, and tracking continues even if your wrist sits low
   near the bottom edge of the frame.
2. Click Calibrate Reach. Point with your index finger at the upper-left
   extent of YOUR comfortable reach in the preview; hold until the prompt
   changes. Then hold at the lower right. These two positions map to the
   corners of the primary screen. You need not reach the camera image edges.
   The dashed rectangle shows the current mapping. Calibration is saved.
3. Open and separate your fingertips once to arm control. Start by pointing
   at something harmless like the title bar of an empty window.

GESTURES
Move         Point comfortably and move your hand. A knuckle-anchored precision
             curve keeps fine text targeting steady while fast sweeps reach far.
Click        Briefly touch thumb to index fingertip and release.
Double-click Pinch thumb + index twice (magnetic target lock keeps the second
             pinch on target), OR pinch thumb + index + middle together once.
Select/drag  Pinch thumb + index and immediately glide to highlight text or
             drag items, or hold stationary briefly (~0.3s), then open to drop.
Right-click  Touch thumb to middle fingertip, then release.
Scroll       Extend only index and middle fingers; fold ring and little
             fingers, keep the thumb clear, then move both fingers up/down.
Pause        Make a closed fist. Open your hand with fingertips apart to resume.

Minimize the window to control other apps. While active, the click-through
Cursor Halo ring around the pointer shows tracking confidence, pinch progress,
and active selection/drag state (toggleable with "Halo: On/Off"). Closing the
window or clicking Stop Camera stops capture and releases a held drag. Losing
sight of the hand past the brief coasting grace window also releases a drag; a
pinch already held when tracking returns cannot start a click.
The camera feed stays on the device. It is not recorded or uploaded.

Pointer smoothing is automatic. Aiming stays steady against brief fingertip
jitter, while sustained small adjustments and quick hand sweeps still move.
Aim first, then pinch; the app holds the target briefly as the fingers close
and checks multiple observations before clicking or dropping. The built-in
FaceTime HD camera uses visible light, not an infrared feed. For more
reliable detection, light your
hand from the front and keep the fingertips visible. If motion still feels
jumpy, recalibrate a wider reach. For missed clicks, separate the fingertips
between pinches. Control maps to the primary display. Windows does not
inject mouse events into protected or higher-privilege desktops.
